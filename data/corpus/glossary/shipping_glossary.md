# International Freight and Trade Glossary

## A

**Ad-valorem freight rate:** A freight charge expressed as a percentage of the cargo's declared customs value, rather than per kg or per CBM. Common in customs duty calculations and in the UNCTAD GTCDIT dataset benchmarks. Example: a 3.5% ad-valorem rate on USD 100,000 of cargo = USD 3,500 freight cost.

**Air waybill (AWB):** The contract of carriage issued by an airline or freight forwarder for air cargo. Non-negotiable document. The master AWB (MAWB) is issued by the airline; a house AWB (HAWB) is issued by the freight forwarder for consolidated cargo. Required for customs clearance at destination.

**All-in rate:** A freight quote that includes all surcharges (fuel, security, handling) in a single figure. Opposite of "base rate plus surcharges." CIP Incoterms quotes are often all-in.

## B

**BAF (Bunker Adjustment Factor):** A surcharge applied to ocean freight rates to account for fluctuations in marine fuel (bunker oil) prices. Expressed as a flat USD amount per TEU or percentage. High oil prices → high BAF. Also called Fuel Surcharge (FSC) in ocean context.

**Bill of Lading (B/L or BOL):** The contract of carriage for ocean freight, issued by the shipping line. Can be a negotiable (original) or non-negotiable (seaway bill) document. Required for letter of credit transactions. Under FOB and CIF, the seller obtains the B/L for the buyer.

**Break-bulk cargo:** Cargo shipped as individual units (bags, bales, drums, crates) rather than in containers. Common for bulk agricultural commodities. Rarely used for health commodities.

## C

**CBM (Cubic Metre):** Unit of volume used for ocean freight LCL rating. LCL rates are expressed per CBM or per tonne (whichever is greater, subject to stowage factor).

**Chargeable weight:** The weight used to calculate air freight charges — the greater of actual gross weight and volumetric weight (L × W × H in cm ÷ 6000). Dense pharmaceutical cargo usually charges on actual weight; bulky but light cargo charges on volumetric weight.

**Customs Value:** The declared value of goods used as the basis for import duty calculation. For ad-valorem duties and ad-valorem freight rates, the customs value is the reference point.

## D

**Demurrage:** A charge levied by the shipping line when a container is not returned to the terminal within the free days allowed after discharge at the destination port. Demurrage accumulates daily and can become significant for shipments delayed in customs.

**Detention:** A charge for keeping a container outside the terminal (e.g. at the consignee's warehouse) beyond the free period. Distinct from demurrage (which applies inside the terminal).

**DG (Dangerous Goods):** Cargo classified under IATA or IMDG (ocean) dangerous goods regulations. Certain pharmaceuticals and diagnostic reagents are classified as DG and require special packaging, labelling, and documentation, adding to freight cost.

## E

**ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival):** The expected arrival date of cargo at the destination port or airport.

**ETD (Estimated Time of Departure):** The expected departure date of the vessel or flight.

## F

**FCL (Full Container Load):** A shipment that occupies an entire container (20ft or 40ft). The shipper pays for the full container regardless of actual fill. Lower per-unit cost for large volumes compared to LCL.

**FEU (Forty-foot Equivalent Unit):** A standard 40-foot shipping container. Two TEUs equal one FEU by volume.

**Freight Forwarder:** An agent who arranges the carriage of goods on behalf of shippers, handling documentation, customs brokerage, consolidation, and carrier booking. USAID SCMS uses contracted freight management organisations (FMOs) as freight forwarders.

**FSC (Fuel Surcharge):** Applied by airlines to cover jet fuel price volatility. Published monthly by IATA and individual carriers. Can add 30–80% to the base airfreight rate during oil price spikes. A major driver of air freight cost variation over time.

## G

**GTCDIT:** The UNCTAD-World Bank Global Transport Costs Dataset for International Trade. Provides bilateral freight rate benchmarks (ad-valorem % and USD/kg) by country pair, commodity group, and mode (air/sea/rail/road) for 2016–2021. Used as a structural lane-level benchmark, not a real-time rate source.

## H

**HS Code (Harmonized System Code):** An international nomenclature for classifying traded products, maintained by the World Customs Organization (WCO). The first 6 digits are internationally standardised; additional digits vary by country. Health commodities: Chapter 30 (pharmaceutical products), Chapter 38 (chemical preparations), Chapter 90 (medical instruments).

**Hub airport:** A major cargo gateway used as a consolidation and distribution point. Key USAID hubs: Johannesburg (JNB), Nairobi (NBO), Accra (ACC), Dakar (DKR).

## I

**INCO term / Incoterm:** One of the 11 standardised trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) under Incoterms 2020, defining the allocation of costs, risks, and responsibilities between buyer and seller in international trade. See individual Incoterms files for details.

**IOR (Importer of Record):** The entity legally responsible for customs clearance and duty payment at the destination country. Under DDP, the seller acts as IOR. Under DAP and all F-terms, the buyer is IOR.

## L

**LCL (Less than Container Load):** Ocean freight for cargo that does not fill a full container. Cargo is consolidated at a Container Freight Station (CFS) with other shippers' cargo. Higher per-CBM cost than FCL but accessible for small volumes. LCL shipments have longer transit times due to consolidation/deconsolidation.

**Lead time:** The total elapsed time from order placement to delivery at the destination. In health commodity supply chains, lead time is a critical KPI. Air freight → 5–14 day lead time; ocean freight → 30–60 days.

## M

**M49 code:** The United Nations geoscheme country/area numeric code, used by UN agencies including UNCTAD in trade datasets. Must be mapped to ISO 3166-1 alpha-2/3 codes for joins with most other datasets. Example: M49 404 = Kenya (ISO: KEN).

**MAWB (Master Air Waybill):** See Air Waybill.

## N

**Non-vessel-operating common carrier (NVOCC):** A freight forwarder or consolidator that issues its own bills of lading but does not operate its own vessels. Common in LCL ocean consolidation.

## O

**On-carriage:** Transport of goods from the destination port or airport to the final delivery point. On-carriage costs are included under DAP, DPU, and DDP, but are the buyer's responsibility under CPT, CFR, CIF, and FOB.

## P

**Pre-carriage:** Transport from the point of origin (seller's factory or warehouse) to the origin port or airport. Under EXW, pre-carriage is the buyer's responsibility. Under all other Incoterms, the seller handles pre-carriage.

**PSS (Peak Season Surcharge):** An additional surcharge applied by ocean carriers during high-demand periods (typically Q3 August–October as Asian manufacturers ship ahead of Western holiday season). Can add USD 200–600/FEU.

## R

**Rate of exchange (ROE):** The currency conversion rate applied to convert freight charges quoted in one currency to the invoice currency. Important for USAID data where charges may be in USD but local currency equivalents vary.

## S

**SCMS (Supply Chain Management System):** The USAID freight management contractor responsible for procurement and delivery of HIV/AIDS health commodities to PEPFAR programme countries from approximately 2006–2015. The SCMS dataset is the source of the USAID training data in this project.

**Shipper:** The party that ships goods — typically the seller (exporter) in international trade.

**SOC (Shipper's Own Container):** A container owned by the shipper rather than the shipping line. Less common; typically used by high-volume shippers.

## T

**TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit):** A standard 20-foot shipping container, the standard unit for measuring container ship capacity and ocean freight volumes.

**THC (Terminal Handling Charge):** Fees charged by port terminals for loading, unloading, and handling containers. Typically charged at both origin and destination ports. THC is separate from the ocean freight rate and is always a buyer or seller cost depending on Incoterms.

**Transit time:** The time elapsed from cargo pick-up at origin to delivery at destination. Air: 1–5 days port-to-port; ocean: 14–45 days.

## U

**UNCTAD (United Nations Conference on Trade and Development):** A UN body that publishes the Global Transport Costs Dataset for International Trade (GTCDIT), among other trade and development statistics. The UNCTAD benchmark in this model reflects structural bilateral freight rate intensities by mode and country pair.

## W

**WCI (World Container Index):** Published by Drewry. Tracks weekly spot container freight rates on 8 major east-west trade lanes. Used as a benchmark for ocean freight market conditions.

**Weight break:** A discount tier in airfreight pricing where the per-kg rate decreases at higher weight thresholds (e.g. lower rate per kg at 100kg+ vs 45kg+). Important for understanding why per-kg freight costs decrease with shipment size in the USAID dataset.

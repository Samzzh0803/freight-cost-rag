# Global Freight Market Context — Q1/Q2 2026

*All figures in this file are sourced from publicly accessible primary indices retrieved 2026-05-04. Lane-specific rates (Europe→Nairobi, etc.) are NOT included here because those require subscription access to Freightos FAX or Xeneta — see africa_corridor_context.md for structural corridor descriptions without fabricated spot rates.*

## Air Cargo Market — Verified Q1 2026 Figures

**IATA cargo tonne-kilometre (CTK) demand (year-on-year):**
- January 2026: +5.6% (source: IATA press release 2026-03-02)
- February 2026: +11.2% international CTK (source: IATA press release 2026-04-01)
- March 2026: -4.8% (contraction, attributed to Middle East disruption impact on schedules)
- Full-year 2026 IATA forecast: +2.6% global CTK growth

**Global air cargo spot rates (WorldACD / Bertling Apr 2026 report):**
- March 2026 average: USD 2.86/kg (highest since December 2024)
- Week 15 (mid-April 2026): USD 3.76/kg (up ~37% year-on-year)
- Yields up 18.9% in March 2026 year-on-year

These are global composite rates across all lanes. Africa-specific lane rates are not publicly available without subscription (Freightos FAX, Xeneta Air). The global composite provides directional context only — African routes typically run at a premium to global averages due to thinner carrier competition.

**What this means for health commodity airfreight:** Global air cargo demand was strong in January–February 2026 and yields were rising. March softened. The structural picture: air freight remains significantly more expensive than pre-pandemic norms, and yield pressure on African routes is higher than global averages.

## Ocean Container Market — Verified Figures (Late April/May 2026)

**Drewry World Container Index (WCI) composite — 8-lane average:**
- As of 30 April 2026: USD 2,216 per 40ft container (FEU)
- Trend: declined 1% in the week to 30 April (third consecutive weekly decline)
- Source: https://www.drewry.co.uk/supply-chain-advisors/supply-chain-expertise/world-container-index-assessed-by-drewry

**Freightos Baltic Index (FBX) — global composite:**
- As of early May 2026: approximately USD 1,933 per 40ft container
- FBX11 (China→Northern Europe): approximately USD 2,707/FEU as of early February 2026 (rose 11% in that period)
- Source: https://www.freightos.com/freightos-baltic-index/

**Context:** Both indices show significant softening from 2024 peaks (FBX exceeded USD 7,000/FEU in mid-2024 driven by Red Sea disruption). By May 2026, rates had normalised toward the USD 2,000–2,500 range — still above the pre-pandemic USD 1,500 norm but no longer at crisis levels.

**Africa-specific ocean lanes (Europe→Mombasa, Europe→Durban):** Not publicly available at lane level from Drewry or FBX without subscription. Structural context in africa_corridor_context.md.

## Red Sea Disruption — Status as of May 2026

Since Q4 2023, most major carriers have avoided Suez Canal transits due to Houthi attacks on commercial shipping. Asia-Europe voyages route via the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10–14 days and fuel cost. This structural disruption is reflected in the elevated WCI/FBX rates relative to pre-2024 norms. European-origin freight to East Africa has a shorter Cape routing penalty than Asia-origin.

## Structural Comparison: USAID SCMS Era vs 2026

The USAID SCMS dataset covers 2006–2015, a period of moderate, relatively stable freight rates. The 2026 market is structurally more expensive for both air and ocean, driven by:
1. Elevated fuel costs above 2010s baseline
2. Post-pandemic structural capacity adjustments
3. Red Sea disruption surcharges on ocean lanes

Model predictions trained on 2006–2015 freight costs will systematically underestimate 2026 market rates. The RAG layer should flag this gap when users ask about current rates.

# Sub-Saharan Africa Freight Corridor Context 2025–2026

*Structural freight context for the key corridors relevant to USAID health commodity delivery. Updated 2026-05-04.*

## Key Air Cargo Hubs and Corridors

Sub-Saharan Africa health commodity airfreight flows through a hub-and-spoke system. The three primary regional hubs are:

**Johannesburg O.R. Tambo International (JNB):** The largest cargo hub in Sub-Saharan Africa. Serves Southern Africa: Zimbabwe, Zambia, Malawi, Mozambique, Botswana, Namibia. Strong cargo connections to Europe (Amsterdam, Frankfurt, London) and the US (Atlanta, New York).

**Nairobi Jomo Kenyatta International (NBO):** Primary hub for East Africa: Uganda, Rwanda, Tanzania, Ethiopia (though Addis has its own hub), Burundi, DRC (partially). Ethiopian Airlines Cargo is a major carrier on African routes operating via Addis Ababa (ADD).

**Accra Kotoka International (ACC):** Hub for West Africa: Ghana, Nigeria (Lagos LOS is also a major direct market), Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire.

## Ocean Freight Corridors to Africa

Key port-to-corridor flows for health commodity ocean freight:

**East Africa (Mombasa port):** Gateway for Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, DRC (eastern). Northern Corridor road: Mombasa → Nairobi → Kampala (Uganda) → Kigali (Rwanda). Transit time from Mombasa to Kampala: 4–7 days by road. Port congestion at Mombasa has historically added 3–10 days wait time.

**East Africa (Dar es Salaam port):** Gateway for Tanzania, Zambia, Malawi, DRC (southeastern). TAZARA rail + Central Corridor road. Slower and less reliable than Mombasa for landlocked destinations.

**Southern Africa (Durban port):** Largest port in Sub-Saharan Africa. Serves South Africa (domestic), Zimbabwe, Zambia (southern route), Botswana. Well-connected to Johannesburg rail and road.

**West Africa (Tema port, Ghana + Apapa, Nigeria):** Serves West African markets. Tema is better-managed; Apapa (Lagos) is notorious for congestion and dwell times of 10–30 days — a significant supply chain risk.

## 2025–2026 Corridor-Specific Issues

**Red Sea/Suez disruption:** Continues to affect Asia-origin ocean freight to East Africa. Cape of Good Hope routing adds 10–14 days vs Suez. European-origin ocean freight to East Africa is less affected (shorter delta for Cape routing).

**Mombasa port efficiency:** World Bank logistics performance data shows improving but still below-benchmark customs clearance times at Mombasa — typically 5–10 days for health commodity clearance vs 1–3 days in developed market ports.

**Landlocked country challenges:** Uganda, Rwanda, Zambia, Malawi, and Zimbabwe all require inland road transport from regional ports. Road transport reliability, fuel availability, and border post clearance remain key lead-time risks.

## Freight Rate Benchmarks for African Corridors

**Important provenance note:** Lane-specific spot rates for Africa-bound air freight (e.g. Europe→Nairobi, US→Nairobi) are not publicly available from the major indices (Freightos FAX, Xeneta Air) without subscription access. The figures below are structural order-of-magnitude ranges drawn from logistics provider published quotes and industry reports — they are directional context, not verified spot rates. Do not cite them as benchmark data in reports.

**Air freight (structural ranges, not verified spot rates):**
- Published logistics provider quotes for US→Kenya: USD 7.80–20.00/kg (wide range reflecting service tier differences, not market rates)
- Global air cargo composite spot rate: USD 2.86–3.76/kg (March–April 2026, WorldACD via Bertling report; this is a global average, African routes typically at a premium)
- Africa routes run at a structural premium to global averages due to thinner carrier competition and longer hauls

**Ocean freight (verified from Drewry WCI / FBX, May 2026):**
- Global WCI composite: USD 2,216/FEU (30 April 2026, Drewry)
- FBX global composite: ~USD 1,933/FEU (early May 2026, Freightos)
- Africa-specific lanes (Europe→Mombasa, Europe→Durban) are not publicly broken out; they typically track 10–30% above global composite on comparable distances

**Road freight (structural, no verified 2026 source):**
- Mombasa→Kampala corridor (~1,000km): industry range of USD 2,000–4,000/FTL trip; highly variable with load, timing, fuel prices, and border conditions
- No public real-time index for African road freight lanes

*Use the air/ocean ranges above as rough directional context only. For actual procurement decisions, obtain quotes from freight forwarders. For model interpretation, the relevant comparison is USAID 2006–2015 rates vs 2026 market — the direction (2026 higher) is clear even without precise lane rates.*

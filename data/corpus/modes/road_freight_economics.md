# Road Freight Economics and Cost Drivers

Road freight (trucking) is the primary mode for last-mile and inland distribution, connecting ports, airports, and warehouses to final delivery points. In Sub-Saharan Africa — the primary destination region for USAID health commodity shipments — road freight is essential for reaching landlocked or remote health facilities after air or ocean arrival at a hub. It is also used as a primary mode for intra-regional cross-border shipments (e.g. South Africa to Zimbabwe, Kenya to Uganda).

## Cost Structures

Road freight is priced per kilometre per tonne, per full truckload (FTL), or per load for part-loads (LTL — Less than Truckload). Costs vary enormously by:
- **Road quality and infrastructure:** Sub-Saharan African roads add cost and transit time due to poor surface conditions, checkpoints, and limited truck maintenance infrastructure.
- **Border crossing delays:** Cross-border trucking in East and Southern Africa can face 1–5 day delays at border posts due to customs, documentation, and informal payments.
- **Fuel prices:** Diesel is the dominant fuel; prices vary significantly across African countries.
- **Lane competition:** Established corridors (Mombasa–Nairobi–Kampala; Durban–Johannesburg–Harare) have better rates due to competition. Remote corridors have limited carrier options.

## Typical Cost Range

- **Sub-Saharan Africa cross-border trucking:** USD 0.05–0.20/kg depending on distance, road conditions, and load factor
- **Intra-country last-mile (capital to district):** USD 0.02–0.10/kg
- **Per km per tonne (rough benchmark):** USD 0.05–0.15/tonne-km for major corridors

These are structural benchmarks; actual rates for health commodity logistics programmes may differ due to contracted rates.

## Road Freight in the USAID Dataset

The USAID dataset labels "Truck" as a mode. Truck shipments represent roughly 0.3–1% of records and are used for: (1) intra-country or short cross-border shipments, (2) last-mile delivery from a hub airport or port to a central medical store, or (3) regional consolidation in Southern/East Africa. In the USAID SCMS dataset, "Truck" records typically originate from South Africa (Johannesburg hub) with destinations in neighbouring countries (Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Malawi).

## The Corridor Logic in Health Commodity Supply Chains

USAID uses a hub-and-spoke model: international freight (primarily air) arrives at regional hub airports (Johannesburg, Nairobi, Accra). Trucking covers the final corridor to the destination country central medical store. This means the freight cost field for air shipments in the dataset covers air only; a separate "last mile" cost may not be captured. Truck records in the dataset typically represent the full surface transport leg for a given shipment, not a supplementary inland leg.

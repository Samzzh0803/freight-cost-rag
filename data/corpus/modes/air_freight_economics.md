# Air Freight Economics and Cost Drivers

Air freight is the fastest mode of international cargo transport, with typical transit times of 1–5 days port-to-port globally. It is also the most expensive — air freight rates per kilogram typically run 4–6× the equivalent ocean freight rate for the same route. Despite high cost, air freight accounts for roughly 35% of global trade value (but under 1% of volume), reflecting its use for time-sensitive, high-value, and perishable commodities.

## Typical Cost Range

Air freight is priced per kilogram, with a standard rate applying to actual weight or volumetric weight (dimensional weight), whichever is greater. Volumetric weight is calculated as (L × W × H in cm) / 6000. For dense commodities like pharmaceuticals and ARVs, actual weight typically governs.

- **Short-haul (intra-regional, e.g. Nairobi to Lusaka):** USD 1.50–3.00/kg
- **Long-haul intercontinental (e.g. Europe/US to Sub-Saharan Africa):** USD 3.00–7.00/kg
- **Rates fluctuate significantly** with fuel prices, demand surges, and capacity constraints.

## Key Cost Components

1. **Base airfreight rate (FAK — Freight All Kinds):** The core rate per kg for the specific O-D pair.
2. **Fuel surcharge (FSC):** Fluctuates with jet fuel prices. Can add 30–60% to the base rate during oil price spikes. IATA publishes monthly fuel surcharge guidance.
3. **Security surcharge (SSC):** Reflects screening and security compliance costs.
4. **Terminal handling charges (THC):** Airport handling at origin and destination.
5. **Customs and documentation fees:** Air waybill preparation, export declaration.
6. **Special handling:** Temperature-controlled (cold chain), dangerous goods (DG) surcharges apply to pharmaceuticals.

## Why Air Freight Dominates USAID Health Commodity Shipments

In the USAID SCMS dataset, approximately 96% of shipments by count are airfreighted. The drivers are:
- **Urgency:** HIV/ARV stockouts are life-threatening; lead time matters more than cost per kg.
- **Commodity value density:** ARVs have high value-to-weight ratios; airfreight cost as a fraction of commodity value is manageable.
- **Cold chain requirements:** Some ARVs and diagnostics require temperature-controlled air shipment.
- **Corridor economics:** Sub-Saharan Africa ocean port infrastructure limits ocean freight viability for landlocked destinations (e.g. Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe). Air reaches these markets directly.

## When Air Freight is Chosen Over Ocean

Air freight is chosen when: (1) lead time is under 7 days, (2) commodity value exceeds ~USD 15/kg and airfreight adds under 10% to commodity cost, (3) ocean infrastructure is absent or unreliable at the destination, or (4) temperature-sensitive products cannot survive weeks-long ocean transit. For routine, high-volume, non-urgent commodity replenishment, ocean freight is preferred due to the 4–6× cost advantage.

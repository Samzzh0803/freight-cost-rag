# FAS — Free Alongside Ship

FAS (Free Alongside Ship) requires the seller to deliver the goods alongside the named vessel at the named port of shipment. Risk passes once the goods are placed alongside the ship. The seller must clear the goods for export. The buyer is responsible for loading costs, the main sea carriage, and all subsequent costs and risks.

FAS is a sea-and-inland-waterway-only rule and is not appropriate for containerised cargo, where goods are handed over at a container terminal rather than alongside a vessel. It is most appropriate for bulk commodities (grain, coal, ore) shipped in break-bulk or bulk vessels.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Delivery alongside vessel | Yes | No |
| Loading onto vessel | No | Yes |
| Main sea carriage | No | Yes |
| Insurance | No obligation | At buyer's discretion |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |

## When to Use FAS

FAS is rarely used for health commodity or pharmaceutical shipments, which are almost exclusively containerised or airfreighted. It is included here for completeness. For USAID commodity flows, FAS would be atypical — DAP, DDP, or CIP is far more common. If encountered in a shipment record coded as FAS, verify it is not a data-entry error for FCA.

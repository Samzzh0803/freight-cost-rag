# FCA — Free Carrier

FCA (Free Carrier) means the seller delivers the goods to a named carrier or another person nominated by the buyer at the seller's premises or another named place. Risk passes to the buyer when the seller hands the goods to the carrier. The seller is responsible for export clearance; the buyer arranges and pays for the main international carriage.

A key Incoterms 2020 update: when FCA is used with a letter of credit requiring an on-board bill of lading, the parties may agree that the buyer instructs their carrier to issue an on-board B/L to the seller after loading — addressing a long-standing documentary credit gap.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Loading at origin | Yes (if seller's premises) | Buyer arranges carrier |
| Main carriage | No | Yes |
| Insurance | No obligation | At buyer's discretion |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Delivery to final destination | No | Yes |

## When to Use FCA

FCA is the most flexible multimodal rule — it works for air, sea, road, and rail, and is appropriate when the seller has a strong logistics presence at origin. It is often preferred over FOB for containerised sea freight because risk transfers before the port (FOB's risk point at ship's rail is outdated for container logistics). For health commodity procurement, FCA is used when the freight forwarder is contracted by the buying entity (e.g. USAID freight management contractor) and the supplier delivers to the forwarder's facility.

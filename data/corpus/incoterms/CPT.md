# CPT — Carriage Paid To

CPT (Carriage Paid To) is the multimodal equivalent of CFR. The seller arranges and pays for carriage to the named place of destination. Risk transfers to the buyer when the goods are handed to the first carrier at origin — not at the destination. Like CFR, there is a split between who pays freight and who bears risk during transit.

CPT works for any mode of transport: air, sea, road, rail, or multimodal. This makes it more versatile than CFR/CIF (which are sea-only). The seller clears goods for export; the buyer handles import clearance.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Carriage to named destination | Yes (pays freight) | No |
| Insurance | No obligation | At buyer's discretion |
| Risk during main carriage | Buyer (transfers at first carrier) | Yes |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Delivery at destination | No (to named place, not door) | Yes |

## When to Use CPT

CPT is appropriate for multimodal or airfreight shipments where the seller arranges the main carriage but the buyer takes risk during transit. It is particularly relevant for health commodity airfreight (the dominant mode in the USAID dataset), where the seller ships air cargo to a named destination airport or freight terminal, pays the airfreight, and the buyer's local agent handles customs and last-mile. When a USAID record is CPT, the quoted freight cost represents the seller-arranged airfreight to destination hub.

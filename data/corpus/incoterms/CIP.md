# CIP — Carriage and Insurance Paid To

CIP (Carriage and Insurance Paid To) is the multimodal equivalent of CIF — carriage and insurance paid by the seller to the named destination, but risk transfers to the buyer when goods are handed to the first carrier at origin. A significant Incoterms 2020 change: CIP now requires ALL RISKS cargo insurance (Institute Cargo Clauses A or equivalent), a higher standard than CIF's minimum Clauses C cover.

CIP is appropriate for any mode: air, sea, road, rail, or multimodal.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Carriage to named destination | Yes | No |
| All-risks cargo insurance | Yes (Clauses A — upgraded in 2020) | Can arrange additional |
| Risk during main carriage | Buyer (but seller provides all-risks insurance) | Yes |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |

## When to Use CIP

CIP is recommended for high-value cargo, including pharmaceuticals and medical commodities, because the all-risks insurance obligation protects against a wider range of perils than CIF. The 2020 upgrade to Clauses A makes CIP particularly suitable for health commodity supply chains. For USAID airfreight shipments, CIP means the supplier pays airfreight plus all-risks insurance to the destination; the local USAID partner handles customs and last-mile. Buyers should confirm the insurance certificate details to ensure coverage for the full shipment value.

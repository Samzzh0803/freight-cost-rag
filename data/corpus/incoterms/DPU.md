# DPU — Delivered at Place Unloaded

DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded) is the only Incoterm that requires the seller to unload the goods at the named destination. The seller bears all costs and risks up to and including unloading at the named place. This is a new term in Incoterms 2020, replacing DAT (Delivered at Terminal). Unlike DAT, DPU can name any place — not just a terminal — as the delivery point.

Import duties and taxes remain the buyer's responsibility under DPU.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Main carriage | Yes | No |
| Destination unloading | Yes | No |
| Import duties and taxes | No | Yes |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Onward delivery after unloading | No | Yes |

## When to Use DPU

DPU is appropriate when the seller has the logistics capability to unload at the destination and the buyer benefits from this service — for example, delivery to a central medical store where the seller's freight agent handles unloading. It provides slightly more seller responsibility than DAP (seller unloads; under DAP the buyer unloads). For health commodity shipments, DPU is used when the freight contractor is obliged to deliver goods off the truck or plane into the receiving warehouse. In the USAID dataset, DPU (or its predecessor DAT) records are rare; DAP is more common.

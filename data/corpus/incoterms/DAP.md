# DAP — Delivered at Place

DAP (Delivered at Place) requires the seller to deliver goods ready for unloading at the named place of destination. The seller bears all costs and risks of transport to the destination — including main carriage, any transit and destination country inland costs — but does not pay import duties or taxes. Import clearance is the buyer's responsibility. Risk transfers to the buyer when the goods are available for unloading at the named destination.

DAP replaced the old DDU (Delivered Duty Unpaid) term from Incoterms 2000. Records in older datasets (including USAID, which covers 2006–2015) coded as DDU are functionally equivalent to DAP.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Main carriage | Yes | No |
| Destination country inland transport | Yes | No |
| Insurance | No obligation | At buyer's discretion |
| Unloading at destination | No (goods ready for unloading) | Yes |
| Import duties and taxes | No | Yes |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |

## When to Use DAP

DAP is one of the most common Incoterms for health commodity shipments to PEPFAR and USAID programme countries, as it places maximum logistics responsibility on the seller/freight contractor — reflecting the reality that recipient countries often lack robust customs infrastructure. In the USAID dataset, DAP (and its predecessor DDU) records indicate that the freight cost includes door-to-named-place delivery costs. DAP freight costs are therefore typically higher than FOB or FCA for the same lane, as they include inland distribution at the destination.

# CFR — Cost and Freight

CFR (Cost and Freight) requires the seller to arrange and pay for the sea carriage to the named port of destination. However, risk transfers to the buyer once the goods are on board the vessel at the port of shipment — not at the destination. This creates a critical split: the seller pays for freight but the buyer bears the risk of loss or damage during transit.

CFR is a sea-and-inland-waterway-only rule. The seller must clear goods for export. Because the seller arranges freight but bears no risk during carriage, the buyer is strongly advised to arrange marine cargo insurance (though not obliged under CFR).

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Loading onto vessel | Yes | No |
| Main sea carriage cost | Yes | No |
| Marine insurance | No obligation (seller pays freight, buyer at risk) | Buyer's choice |
| Risk during sea carriage | Buyer (transfers on board at origin) | Yes |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Delivery from destination port | No | Yes |

## When to Use CFR

CFR is appropriate when the seller has better access to freight rates (e.g. regular volume with carriers) but the buyer has import and inland logistics capability at the destination. In health commodity contexts, it is used when the supplier arranges shipping but the receiving country's supply chain entity manages port clearance and delivery. Note that the cost embedded in the shipment price includes ocean freight but not insurance — buyers using CFR should budget for insurance separately.

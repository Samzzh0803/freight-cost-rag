# CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight

CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) is identical to CFR except that the seller must also procure minimum cargo insurance against the buyer's risk of loss or damage during carriage. The seller pays freight and insurance to the named destination port, but risk transfers to the buyer once goods are on board at the port of shipment — same risk transfer point as CFR.

CIF is a sea-and-inland-waterway-only rule. The insurance obligation under CIF is minimum cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C or equivalent) — buyers with high-value cargo should consider stipulating broader cover or arranging their own insurance above the minimum.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Main sea carriage cost | Yes | No |
| Minimum cargo insurance | Yes (Clauses C or equivalent) | Can arrange additional cover |
| Risk during sea carriage | Buyer (but seller provides insurance) | Yes |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Delivery from destination port | No | Yes |

## When to Use CIF

CIF is widely used in commodity trade and is common in international health commodity procurement. It is one of the more frequent Incoterms in the USAID shipment dataset. When a USAID record is coded CIF, the freight cost field includes sea freight and minimum insurance — the shipping cost is bundled with the product cost. This affects the interpretation of freight cost ratios: CIF freight cost includes insurance premium. For freight cost modelling, CIF records should be treated as sea freight (mode = Ocean) with insurance bundled in.

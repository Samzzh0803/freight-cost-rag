# FOB — Free On Board

FOB (Free On Board) is one of the most widely recognised Incoterms. The seller delivers the goods on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment. Risk transfers to the buyer once the goods are on board the vessel. The seller is responsible for export clearance and loading costs up to and including on-board delivery.

FOB is a sea-and-inland-waterway rule. It is not appropriate for containerised cargo (where the correct term is FCA), but it remains extremely common in trade practice for containerised goods due to historical familiarity. In practice, risk often transfers before the ship's rail for containers — a well-known mismatch that Incoterms 2020 guidance explicitly flags.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Loading onto vessel | Yes | No |
| Main sea carriage | No | Yes |
| Marine insurance | No obligation | At buyer's discretion |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Delivery to final destination | No | Yes |

## When to Use FOB

FOB is appropriate for break-bulk, bulk, or non-containerised sea cargo. For containerised shipments, FCA is technically more correct. In health commodity supply chains, FOB is used when the buying entity (e.g. USAID or its freight contractor) arranges the ocean freight and wants the supplier to deliver goods on board. The freight cost in the USAID dataset under FOB terms represents the buyer-side carriage cost, not included in the supplier's quoted price.

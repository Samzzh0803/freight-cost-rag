# EXW — Ex Works

EXW places the maximum obligation on the buyer and minimum on the seller. The seller fulfils their obligation by making the goods available at their premises (factory, warehouse, or other named place). The seller does not need to load the goods onto any collecting vehicle, nor clear the goods for export. From the moment the goods are available at the named place, all costs and risks transfer to the buyer.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | No | Yes |
| Loading at origin | No (seller makes available) | Yes |
| Carriage to port | No | Yes |
| Main carriage (air/sea/road) | No | Yes |
| Insurance | No obligation | At buyer's risk |
| Import clearance | No | Yes |
| Delivery to final destination | No | Yes |

## When to Use EXW

EXW is appropriate when the buyer has strong logistics capability in the seller's country and wants full control over the shipment. It is rarely appropriate for international trade where the buyer lacks presence in the export country, because export customs clearance falls entirely on the buyer. For USAID/health commodity shipments, EXW is uncommon — DAP or DDP is more typical since the buyer (receiving country) often lacks export clearance capability. EXW is most practical for domestic or intra-EU trade.

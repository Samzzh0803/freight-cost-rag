# DDP — Delivered Duty Paid

DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) places the maximum obligation on the seller and minimum on the buyer. The seller is responsible for delivering the goods to the named destination, cleared for import, with all duties and taxes paid. The seller bears all costs and risks from origin through to delivery at the buyer's door. The buyer need only receive the goods.

DDP is the most seller-heavy Incoterm. It requires the seller to be able to import into the destination country — including paying duties, VAT, and any other import taxes. This can be logistically and legally complex, particularly in countries where foreign entities cannot legally act as importer of record.

## Buyer and Seller Obligations

| Obligation | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Export clearance | Yes | No |
| Main carriage | Yes | No |
| Destination inland transport | Yes | No |
| Import clearance | Yes | No |
| Import duties and taxes | Yes | No |
| Unloading at destination | No (ready for unloading) | Yes |

## When to Use DDP

DDP is used when the buyer wants zero logistics responsibility and the seller/freight contractor has the capability to act as importer of record in the destination country. In health commodity supply chains (e.g. PEPFAR shipments), DDP is used when the programme mandates full door delivery with duty clearance — often for countries with complex import regimes. DDP freight costs are the highest of all Incoterms for the same lane, as they include import duties, taxes, and all inland costs. When a USAID record is coded DDP, the freight cost field may include duty and tax components — the freight cost will appear elevated relative to DAP for the same lane and weight.
